# Strava_Fitness_Project
Behavioral analytics project using Strava fitness tracker data to uncover patterns between physical activity, sleep, sedentary behavior, and calorie expenditure through Python, SQL, and Power BI.
This project was my attempt to understand how everyday lifestyle behavior reflects through fitness tracking data. Instead of only looking at numbers, I wanted to study the patterns behind them — how activity, movement, sleep, sedentary time, and calorie burn are connected in real life.

**The analysis was built using fitness tracker records containing columns like:**
daily steps,
calories burned,
activity intensity,
sleep duration,
sedentary minutes,
weight,
BMI,
and hourly activity logs.
Using these variables, I explored behavioral trends and relationships between physical activity and lifestyle habits.

**Tools Used**

Python — for data cleaning and exploratory analysis

SQL — for querying and organizing the data

Power BI — for dashboard building and visualization

What I Worked On

The first step was cleaning and preparing the raw data,
removed duplicate records,
converted timestamp formats,
handled inconsistent values,
and created additional calculated measures like sleep efficiency and total sleep hours.
After preprocessing, I analyzed how different lifestyle variables interact with each other and visualized the findings in Power BI.

**Key Insights****

**Activity & Calories****
Daily activity showed a strong positive relationship with calorie expenditure. As step counts increased, calories burned generally increased as well.

**Behavioral Timing Patterns**
Most activity peaks happened during late afternoon and evening hours, suggesting that users were more active after work or during dedicated workout hours.

**Sedentary Lifestyle & Sleep**
Users with higher sedentary behavior often appeared to have lower sleep duration, indicating a possible imbalance in routine and lifestyle habits.

**Sleep vs Physical Activity**
One of the most interesting findings was that higher activity levels did not necessarily guarantee better sleep duration. Sleep behavior seemed to depend on multiple lifestyle factors beyond movement alone.

**Weight vs Activity**
People with similar body weight often showed very different activity levels, highlighting that behavior and routine play a much bigger role than physical attributes alone.

**Why I Built This Project**

I wanted to create a project that felt more human than just dashboards and KPIs. Fitness data is interesting because it captures everyday habits without people even realizing it.
**This project helped me improve my skills in:**
data analysis,
visualization,
SQL querying,
behavioral analytics,
and storytelling with data.
It also taught me how much human behavior can be understood through patterns hidden inside simple activity records.
